Falls are a super common job for emergency services.

things to keep in mind:
- despite often being low equity, there are the chance for life threatening consequences and origins of falls.

Consequences
- RHABDO and AKI and Hyperkalemia
- Rib Fractures and Pneumo
- TBI
- Pelvic or (neck of) femur fractures
- Hypothermia, Dehydration
Causes
- Strokes
- Cardiac events
- Infection
- 

Following our general approach, this is what an approach would look like with HX and Ax filled in.


PRIMARY SURVEY
- Dangers - consider mechanical causes  
Introduction
- Introduce yourself
Chief Concern
- Ask on open-ended question
Pertinent Questions/Examinations
- Head strikes
- Spinal clearance
- Blood thinning medications
- Long lie
- Pain
VSS
- After getting unconcerning vital signs (and a spinal clearance), consider repositing your patient to make them more comfortable.
- Dont miss: BGL, pupils, 12 lead ECG
Hx of Chief Concern
- Site
	- Consider the place that the fall occured, obstacles involved, body parts fallen onto
- Onset
	- Ask directly what they think may have caused the fall, and determine if in your opinion this is a mechanical or medical cause, you can ask if falling is usual or unusual, and examine any acute changes in mobility. Also consider any clues in the environment that make you lean toward a mechanical cause, ie rugs, lack of handles, slippery flooring, etc. or a medical cause, alcohol/drugs
- Character
	- Ensure you have a clear idea of the mechanism, including the height, surface, obstacles, attempt to break the fall, any prodrome or funny feelings immediately prior to or after the fall. 
	- Consider if the fall was witnessed and ask for bystanders to corroborate the story.
- Radiation
	- nil
- Associated Symptoms
	- You can leave this to later but Consider dangerous areas and assocaited smptoms, ie neuro alterations for head strikes, chest pain/SOB for ribs fractures/ pneumothorax, pelvic pain, NOF pain, 
- Timing
	- Consider how long the paitnet was on the floor for
- Exacerbating/Reliving
	- nil
- Severity
	- nil

Medical History
- Gather a medical history, look out for causes:
	- Parkinsons/strokes
	- Arhytmias
	- Seizure history
	- Prescribed Depressants
- And consequences:
	- Blood thinners
	- Osteoporosis
	- Chronic steroid use
Focused Exam/History
- Most of the history will be completed in the SOCRATES characterisation of the fall, but consider examining NSA more closely, and then covering bases for causes of a fall that may have gone unnoticed, key differentals can be found in an AMS framework such as AEIOU TIPS. Let those differentials, and the exclusion of acute lifethreats guide your focused examination and history
- Ensure you characterise typical mobility and falls history
Risks
- Examine anything in the social history that may present a redflag needing follow up, this is particularly importnatn for leaving patients at home, ie living alone, capacity to call for help, acute and sudden change in mobility - but is also important for patients that you will tranpsort, as it will catalyse important interventions for their life when they return.
Recap
- Discuss your findings with the patient, team and family.
Plan
- Decide on a course of action.
- Always get pateitns to demonstrate their baseline mobility to you and any carers/relatives who are familiar with them before you leave them at home.
