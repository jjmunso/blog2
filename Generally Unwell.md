The generally unwell patient proposes a particularly difficult case for paramedics, who are often more well aquainted with those who have acute life-threats or complaints associated with specific syndromes.
This approach benefits from similar principles as the approach to the altered mental status patient, or the approach to non-communinicative patient.

Remember to search for lifethreatening features in a framework that is systematic, before moving in to a history an examination that attempts to characterise their current state. These patients can be extremly difficult to adequately represent despite often low stakes its important that you are thorough in using einvironmental clues, family on scene, etc. in order to best select a referal pathway and advocate for your patient down those paths. 

Another key thing to keep in mind when approaching these patients is to establish the deveiation from baseline in mangitiude and direction. This may be the only concerning fefature of your examination/history, but is cruical. Just because a paitent has no red flag symptoms, does not mean they are safe to leave in the community.

Useful tools
- Speaking loudly, clearly and in simple terms
- Being straightforward an even demanding
