Takeaways
- Ventilations are commonly done incorrectly and the person assigned to ventilations should, much like the person assigned to compressions, have nothing else to think about besides the quality of their intervention
- Hyperventilating, at a rate and volume excessive for the patient is common.
- Build a troubleshooting system for a patient that is difficult to ventilate

Points to think about
- What is the difference between the ventilations you provide with a BVM and the way respirations naturally occur? Physically what is causing the gas to move, and what implications might this have for our patient?
- Where do you draw the line for begining or ceasing ventilations?
- How are you going to be an effective ventilator and avoid contributing to the harm caused by innappropriate ventilation?
- What special cases have not been considered in this article that you should be familar with for cases you've been to or heard of?
